Product Exploration · Two-Sided Marketplace · Trust & Liquidity

Gatherly

Designing a managed event marketplace around a harder promise than discovery: helping people confidently find, confirm and complete event-rental transactions.

Project TypeSelf-initiated Concept
RoleProduct Manager
Initial MarketLagos · Event Rentals
StatusHypothesis-led · Not yet validated
Long-term product vision
One place to find and book the things, spaces and professionals that bring an event together.
01 · The starting hypothesis

The visible problem is finding vendors. The deeper problem may be completing a reliable booking.

Planning an event can involve discovering vendors across referrals and social platforms, checking prices and availability through messages, coordinating several suppliers, making payments and hoping every commitment holds on the event date.

Initial hypothesis → Event hosts need a more reliable way to source and book rental inventory.

This case study is a product exploration, not validated user research. The problems, segments and design decisions below are hypotheses I would test with renters, vendors and professional event planners before committing to a full build.

02 · Marketplace thinking

Supply is useless without demand. Demand is useless without usable supply.

Gatherly is a two-sided marketplace. Product success depends on more than acquiring users: the right inventory has to be available for the right customer, date, quantity and location at the moment they need it.

Demand side

Occasional event hosts may value convenience, trustworthy vendors and fewer coordination headaches. Professional planners may have an even stronger use case because they source repeatedly across multiple events.

Supply side

Rental vendors need qualified demand without losing control of inventory, external bookings, delivery constraints, deposits and the operational realities of fulfilment.

Marketplace liquidity is not “How many vendors are listed?” It is: when someone needs something, how often can the marketplace actually get them to a completed transaction?
03 · Choosing a wedge

Start dense, not broad.

A nationwide marketplace with scattered supply can look large while still failing most searches. I would begin in Lagos and concentrate supply before expanding geographically or across every event category.

01One geography

Start in Lagos to improve the chance that relevant inventory and customers are close enough to transact.

02Rental inventory first

Begin with physical event rentals rather than launching equipment, spaces and professional services simultaneously.

03Test high-utility categories

Candidate starting categories include seating & tables, sound & AV, and tents/canopies — to be validated through research rather than assumed.

04Expand when liquidity earns it

Event spaces and service professionals can enter later once the core transaction works and demand supports broader verticals.

Customers may prefer discovery by event type — such as Wedding, Birthday or Corporate — rather than only by inventory taxonomy. That is a navigation hypothesis to test, not an established behaviour.
04 · A high-frequency segment

Event planners may be more than vendors.

My initial instinct was to include event planners and coordinators as a future service category. Looking at marketplace frequency revealed another possibility: planners may also be high-value demand-side users because they source inventory repeatedly for different clients.

Occasional host

May organise an event infrequently. Needs confidence, convenience and a simpler way to coordinate rentals.

Professional planner

May source across many events. Could value repeat ordering, preferred vendors, consolidated booking records and reliable fulfilment more frequently.

Hypothesis to validate: professional planners could be both a future supply category and one of Gatherly's highest-frequency buyer segments.
05 · The inventory problem

Reliable confirmation beats fake instant availability.

Rental inventory can be booked through WhatsApp, phone calls, referrals or walk-ins. Unless every external booking reaches Gatherly, an “available” badge can become wrong immediately.

01 · REQUEST

Customer requests inventory

Date, location, quantity and rental period are submitted against the vendor's last recorded inventory.

Demand signal
02 · CONFIRM

Vendor verifies availability

The vendor receives a time-limited request and confirms the quantity they can actually fulfil.

Reliability
03 · RESERVE

Inventory is blocked

Once confirmed and booked, that quantity is reserved for the date in the platform inventory calendar.

Inventory integrity
04 · UPDATE

External bookings are recorded

Vendors can record off-platform reservations so availability does not silently drift away from reality.

Operational discipline
Product trade-off
A slower trustworthy transaction is more valuable than a fast booking flow built on inaccurate inventory.
06 · Trust as a system

Don't make disputes depend on who tells the better story.

Rental marketplaces have a trust problem after payment too. If a vendor claims items returned damaged and the customer says they arrived that way, the platform needs evidence designed into the transaction.

01Dispatch evidence

Vendor uploads condition photos before items leave. Bulk identical inventory uses overview photos plus close-ups of known damage rather than photographing every unit.

02Delivery confirmation

Customer confirms receipt and can document the condition of items as delivered.

03Return evidence

Condition is documented again at pickup/return, creating a before-and-after record.

04Deposit resolution

A refundable damage deposit can be released when return is confirmed or held while a documented dispute is reviewed.

Trust shouldn't depend on who tells the more convincing story. Design evidence into the transaction.
07 · When fulfilment fails

A refund does not put chairs at tomorrow's wedding.

If a vendor cancels close to the event, returning the customer's money solves the financial transaction but may not solve the customer's actual problem. Gatherly should offer a choice rather than assuming one recovery path fits everyone.

Option A · Full refund

The customer can end the transaction and receive a refund if they have changed plans or already sourced elsewhere.

Option B · Replacement assistance

If requested, Gatherly searches for comparable inventory matching the date, location and quantity. The customer approves any replacement before rebooking.

Reliability should become visible.

Over time, fulfilment rate, confirmation speed and completed rentals may be more decision-useful than a generic star rating. Exact guarantees, penalties or platform-funded price differences would require validated unit economics before becoming policy.

08 · Why stay on-platform?

Discovery gets customers in. Operational value gives them a reason to stay.

Once a planner and vendor trust each other, both may be tempted to move repeat transactions to WhatsApp and avoid marketplace fees. Preventing contact is brittle. Gatherly needs to remain useful after discovery.

Booking records

Confirmed orders, dates, quantities, payments and receipts remain organised in one place.

Transaction protection

Condition evidence, deposits, fulfilment history and dispute records continue to reduce risk even with a familiar vendor.

Replacement support

When a known vendor cannot fulfil, the marketplace can help source alternatives rather than leaving the planner alone with the failure.

Planner workflow — later

Preferred vendors, repeat orders and a multi-event workspace could deepen value if research confirms planners as a strong repeat segment.

09 · MVP

Prove the transaction before building the event ecosystem.

The first version should test whether concentrated supply plus reliable confirmation and transaction protection can produce completed rentals customers trust enough to repeat.

MUST

Search & request

Browse focused rental inventory by date/location and submit quantity-based booking requests.

Discovery · Intent
MUST

Vendor confirmation

Confirm actual availability before payment/reservation and maintain a simple inventory calendar.

Liquidity · Reliability
MUST

Managed transaction

Booking record, payment/deposit flow, condition evidence and basic cancellation/dispute handling.

Trust · Fulfilment
NOT YET

Everything else

No nationwide launch, instant-booking promise, giant category catalogue, full event-space marketplace or complex planner suite in MVP.

Scope discipline
10 · Research & pilot plan

Validate the problem before scaling the marketplace.

I would start with a deliberately small research sample weighted slightly toward demand, then use a tightly managed pilot to observe actual marketplace behaviour.

01Problem interviews

Begin with 3 renters and 2 rental vendors, then deliberately include professional planners in the next round to test frequency and operational pain.

02Test the real pain

Determine whether the dominant problem is discovery, trust, availability, coordination, pricing, fulfilment — or something the concept currently misses.

03Seed concentrated supply

Recruit a small set of reliable Lagos vendors in a few high-utility categories rather than chasing a large listing count.

04Concierge the first transactions

Manually support early booking requests, confirmations and failures to learn where the workflow breaks before automating it.

11 · What I would measure

The metric that matters is not listings. It's successful matching and fulfilment.

These are proposed pilot measures, not product results.

Request → confirm

What percentage of booking requests can vendors actually fulfil?

Confirm → book

Do confirmed requests convert into paid/reserved transactions?

Fulfilment

What percentage of booked rentals arrive as agreed without vendor cancellation or major failure?

Repeat

Do customers — especially planners — return to transact through Gatherly again?

Supporting signals would include confirmation time, replacement success, dispute rate, vendor cancellation rate, inventory accuracy, category/location search success and repeat on-platform booking after buyer-vendor familiarity develops.
12 · Longer-term vision

Rentals are the wedge. The event ecosystem is the opportunity.

If the core marketplace proves reliable, Gatherly could expand from rental inventory into event spaces and service professionals such as planners/coordinators. Each vertical has different availability, pricing and fulfilment mechanics, so expansion should follow demonstrated demand rather than launch-day ambition.

Things → Spaces → Professionals

The vision is broad. The entry point is intentionally narrow: earn trust in one transaction type, build local liquidity, then expand the system.

13 · Reflection

A marketplace isn't a catalogue. It's a promise that both sides can actually transact.

“The hardest marketplace problem isn't getting inventory onto a screen. It's making supply, demand, trust and operations meet at the right moment.”

I began with an event-rental marketplace idea and quickly realised that discovery alone would not create enough value. Inventory accuracy, fulfilment, damage evidence, cancellation recovery and repeat-booking incentives all shape whether a transaction can be trusted.

The strongest lesson was scope discipline. The long-term vision can include rentals, spaces and professionals, but the MVP should prove a dense, reliable rental transaction first. The next step is not more features — it is research that challenges whether the proposed problem and wedge are real.

The product challenge
Nestuge could get members into a paid community. The harder problem was giving creators and members enough reason to keep the relationship on Nestuge.
01 · Context

The transaction worked. The relationship moved elsewhere.

Nestuge Hubs sits within a broader creator monetization ecosystem. Creators can sell access to communities, courses and digital products, but community activity could continue on WhatsApp or Telegram after a member joined.

01Creator sells access

A creator monetizes knowledge or community through Nestuge.

02Member joins

Payment and access are handled on-platform.

03Engagement moves out

Conversation, support and events can shift to external tools.

04Visibility weakens

Nestuge has less context about community health and post-purchase engagement.

02 · Discovery

I researched the category to find the gap — not to copy a competitor.

The work combined direct observation of Hubs as a creator and member with competitive analysis across four community products.

Circle

Feature-complete community experience with rich engagement mechanics.

Gamms

Strategically overlapping creator monetization and community product.

Swarm

Engagement-first approach focused on active member participation.

Mighty Networks

Community platform at scale with deeper engagement infrastructure.

The useful question wasn't “Which competitor has the most features?” It was “Which gaps matter most for Nestuge's own product strategy?”
03 · Opportunity

Research surfaced opportunities around native events, member discovery, analytics, engagement tooling and draft recovery. Prioritization helped separate strategic value from what could realistically be tackled within the internship window.

Strategic priority ≠ implementation priority.

Native Event Hosting ranked first in the broader feature priority because creators were relying on external meeting tools. But with limited internship time, the work moved to a more contained implementation problem: Auto-Save & Draft Recovery. The lesson was not to confuse “most strategically important” with “best thing to implement next under current constraints.”

04 · From PRD to delivery

I broke Auto-Save into implementable product work.

The solution expanded beyond “save every few seconds.” It covered persistence, recovery, feedback, navigation safety, network resilience and safe deletion for both creators and members.

User edits→Background save→Save-state feedback→Network fails? Preserve + retry→User returns→Restore or discard

05 · Product details

The edge cases were part of the product, not an afterthought.

Selected requirements show how the feature was designed around failure, ambiguity and user trust.

No silent loss

Failed server saves preserve a local copy and trigger retries rather than discarding work.

No silent overwrite

If two device sessions create conflicting drafts, the user chooses which version to keep.

No accidental publish

Recovered content never publishes automatically; recovery returns control to the user.

Accessible feedback

Save states use readable labels and screen-reader announcements without stealing keyboard focus.

06 · Measuring success

What I would measure after launch.

The internship ended before I could evaluate post-launch outcomes, so these are proposed success measures — not claimed results.

Recovery success

Share of interrupted sessions where users successfully recover a saved draft.

Content completion

Whether fewer creator/member editing sessions end without completion after interruptions.

Save reliability

Save failure rate, retry success and frequency of local fallback usage.

User friction

Time lost to rework, repeated editing and abandonment after connectivity or browser failures.

07 · Reflection

What this project changed about how I work.

“More research isn't automatically better research. Research is valuable when it helps the team make a decision.”

Early in the project, I was eager to prove the depth of my work and researched more broadly than the decision required. Looking back, I would frame the research earlier around the specific questions the team needed answered, then stop once the evidence was sufficient to make the next product decision.

I also learned to separate strategic priority from implementation sequence. Native Event Hosting could be the stronger strategic opportunity while a smaller, well-scoped reliability feature became the more realistic piece of work to move forward within a constrained internship timeline.

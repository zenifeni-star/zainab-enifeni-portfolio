Rentwise.

Designing for the decision before the deposit: helping Nigerian renters compare real move-in costs and unresolved inspection questions, without building another listing marketplace.

01 · The problem space

The rent quote isn't the whole decision.

Published reporting describes renters encountering repeated inspection charges, unclear fee stacks and frustrating property searches. But these reports don't prove that another app is needed. The product challenge was to identify a narrower workflow that might improve the decision itself.

How might we help a renter compare homes found anywhere, understand their total upfront commitment, and identify what remains unverified before they pay?
02 · Product strategy

Choose a narrow wedge, not another marketplace.

Target user

Active Lagos house-hunters comparing two or more properties and influencing the payment decision. First-time renters and relocators remain secondary hypotheses.

Alternatives to beat

WhatsApp chats, phone notes, spreadsheets, agents, referrals and property portals. A new product must beat the convenience of these existing habits.

Core job

Keep rental costs, observations and outstanding questions in one private, structured decision workspace.

Deliberate exclusions

No property listings, escrow, agent verification, fraud detection, payment collection or automated legal conclusions.

03 · How I approached it

From evidence to a bounded release.

Discover

Reviewed public reporting, professional guidance and data-protection obligations. Separated evidence from assumptions; drafted a 12–15 participant interview protocol.

Define

Framed the problem around fragmented pre-payment decisions rather than broad housing discovery. Identified source independence and honest uncertainty as differentiators to test.

Prioritize

Selected the smallest coherent loop: save a property → record costs → inspect → compare. Rejected features that implied unearned trust or substantial operational complexity.

Build

Produced a PRD, acceptance criteria, risk register, validation gates, and a mobile-responsive authenticated prototype on AppDeploy.

Learn next

Interview active renters, run moderated usability sessions, audit privacy and authorization, then decide whether to continue, pivot or stop.

04 · The shipped MVP

Four practical decisions, one workspace.

01 / Property records

Save options found through any source, track the area, source and current decision status.

02 / Real upfront cost

Manually itemize rent, agency, agreement, caution, service, moving and other costs; calculate the total without assumed percentages.

03 / Inspection checks

Record ten inspection and verification observations as unknown, looks good or concern—without claiming independent verification.

04 / Side-by-side compare

Compare total upfront amounts, unanswered checks and concerns; export records as JSON.

05 · Outcomes and limitations

A deployed prototype. Not yet product-market fit.

AppDeploy reported the initial deployment ready with no frontend or backend errors in its first checks. That is a technical milestone—not a validated adoption result. No interviews, retention results, user counts, conversion metrics or safety outcomes are claimed.

Current evidence boundary: Secondary research supports the relevance of opaque rental costs and inspection concerns. Whether Rentwise changes user behavior remains untested. The product does not verify listings, agents, landlords, documents or payment recipients.

Success measures proposed for the pilot

  • Two-property activation within seven days.
  • Completion of at least one meaningful comparison.
  • Unassisted cost-comparison task success in usability testing.
  • Correct user understanding that the app provides no safety certification.
  • No critical unauthorized data exposure.
06 · Reflection

The strongest feature may be restraint.

Housing decisions are high-stakes. A misleading “verified” badge can be more harmful than no badge at all. This project forced me to distinguish useful organization from unsupported guarantees, and to treat research, privacy and trust as product requirements rather than decorative promises.

Next decision: test whether the structured workflow outperforms existing WhatsApp and spreadsheet habits for active house-hunters. If it doesn't, simplify or stop.

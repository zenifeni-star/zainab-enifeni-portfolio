The product challenge
How do you make AI fast enough to be useful in healthcare — without asking users to trust it beyond what it should do?
01 · Context

The gap wasn't getting a lab result. It was understanding what came next.

Clinsights was designed for Nigerians who receive laboratory results but may not have quick or affordable access to a doctor to explain them. The MVP combined instant, plain-language AI interpretation with an optional ₦2,500 review from a verified Nigerian doctor.

01Upload a result

Patients can upload a PDF or image — even before creating an account.

02Review extracted values

OCR structures the report and flags uncertain values for patient review.

03Understand the result

AI explains the values in plain language and assigns a risk level.

04Escalate to a doctor

A patient can pay for a verified doctor's human review and follow-up chat.

02 · Product principle

AI for speed. Doctors for judgment.

The MVP was intentionally bounded. AI could translate and explain; it was not positioned to diagnose, prescribe medication or replace clinical judgment. Human review remained part of the product rather than an afterthought.

What AI was for

Fast, plain-language interpretation; structured value breakdowns; risk communication; and non-prescriptive next steps.

Where humans mattered

Verified doctors could review the AI output, correct the risk assessment where necessary, explain the result and guide the patient on appropriate next steps.

The goal wasn't to make AI look like a doctor. It was to use AI to shorten the distance between confusion and informed human judgment.
03 · Product judgment

Some friction needed to disappear. Some friction needed to stay.

One of the strongest tensions in the product was deciding where speed improved the experience — and where additional checks protected the user.

Remove unnecessary friction

Upload before login. A patient could submit a lab result before authentication. A temporary session preserved the file and linked it to the account after sign-up or login, reducing friction before the user reached the product's core value.

Preserve protective friction

Verify before medical access. Doctors were required to be verified before activation. Trust-sensitive actions were intentionally gated rather than optimized for the fewest possible steps.

Good UX isn't always fewer steps.

In a high-consequence product, the better question is: Which friction blocks value, and which friction protects trust? Clinsights needed both answers at once.

04 · Designing for uncertainty

Failure and uncertainty were part of the product requirements.

The flows accounted for imperfect OCR, AI uncertainty, doctor availability, authentication failures and service-level breaches rather than designing only for the happy path.

OCR uncertainty stays visible

Low-confidence extracted values are flagged instead of quietly presented as certain.

Patient corrections don't erase history

A patient's suggested correction is stored separately; the original OCR value remains intact.

AI output has boundaries

The interpretation avoids diagnosis language, drug names and dosages, keeping the MVP on the guidance side of the clinical boundary.

Doctor access is verified

Only verified doctors enter the matching pool, with patient preferences and availability shaping assignment.

Service promises have fallbacks

If a doctor does not accept within the defined window, the product includes fallback and refund logic rather than leaving the patient waiting indefinitely.

Privacy is a product requirement

Patient data access, secure sessions and encrypted lab-result handling were specified as core requirements, not polish for later.

06 · System thinking

Three users. One connected trust system.

The product had to work across patient, doctor and admin experiences. Each role had a different job, but failures in one side could weaken trust across the whole service.

Patient

Upload → understand → verify extracted values → request human review → receive guidance.

Doctor

Receive structured cases → review AI context → submit clinical explanation → follow up with the patient.

Super Admin

Verify doctor credentials → monitor cases and SLAs → handle disputes → watch product health.

System

OCR → AI interpretation → payment → matching → notifications → fallbacks and case lifecycle.

07 · Measuring the MVP

Success needed both growth metrics and safety guardrails.

The PRD defined targets for the MVP. These were planning targets — not post-launch results.

5,000

Q1 target for completed AI lab interpretations — the proposed North Star metric.

75%+

Target upload completion rate, measuring whether users reach the core value.

12%

Target conversion from AI interpretation to paid doctor review.

80%+

Target doctor agreement rate — a guardrail for the quality of AI interpretation.

Other PRD targets included 20%+ repeat upload rate, doctor response under 30 minutes and complaint rate below 3%.

08 · Reflection

What this project taught me about product friction.

“Remove the friction that delays value. Keep the friction that protects trust.”

Clinsights changed how I think about “seamless” experiences. Upload-before-login makes sense because authentication shouldn't prevent someone from reaching the product's core value. Doctor verification makes sense for the opposite reason: reducing those steps could make the product easier to use while making the healthcare experience less trustworthy.

I also learned to think beyond screens. Authentication, OCR confidence, AI boundaries, matching, SLAs and admin controls are interconnected product decisions. A healthcare experience can look simple to the patient only because the system underneath has accounted for complexity.
